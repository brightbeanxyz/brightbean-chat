{% load i18n %} {% comment %} The sidebar — the product's whole chrome. There is no top header: the workspace switcher, the primary navigation, notifications and the account menu all live here. 240px on a desktop, collapsing to 60px, and an off-canvas drawer below `lg`. **The three legs of the anti-flash contract.** The collapse is persisted, so a page can start in either of two widths and the wrong one flashes before Alpine boots. Three pieces stop that, and they only work together: a pre-paint script in base.html's head stamping `sidebar-is-collapsed` on (leg 1), the `.sidebar-initial` width mirror (leg 2, in base.html's head for the width and in styles.css for everything else), and the `x-init` below, which strips both markers the moment Alpine takes over (leg 3). Remove one and you get either a flash on every load or a sidebar frozen at its pre-paint width forever — `.sidebar-initial`'s width is unlayered and beats the `lg:w-*` utility Alpine binds. TestTheSidebar fails the page if any leg goes missing. **One copy of the nav, still.** The drawer does not break the invariant the old bottom tab bar existed to protect: it is this same
, repositioned by a media query and slid in with a transform. A *second* nav would mean a second `nav-badge-inbox`, and an out-of-band swap only ever finds the first — apps/notifications/tests/test_shell.py forbids it. **`settings_groups`.** A settings page draws its nav HERE, in place of the product's, rather than in a second column beside it — the two-column shell put 232px of settings rows next to 240px of app rows the reader had just left. The argument comes from the layout (see layouts/settings.html), because "this is a settings page" is exactly what a layout knows and nothing else does. One

A `settings_mode` flag beside it read better and had a silent failure: a layout passing the flag but naming the rows wrong — `settings_nav_groups`, which is what the context key is actually called — drew a sidebar with a back button, no rows and no error. Django resolves an unknown variable to the empty string, so nothing could raise. With the rows as the switch, the two cannot disagree: no rows, no settings mode. **The bell is on both sets of rows, and that is what keeps its badge honest.** Studio's settings layouts override a `sidebar_nav` block while base.html goes on rendering the poll outside it, so the 60s out-of-band swap aims at a badge id the page no longer has — what partials/_nav_badge_sinks.html existed to paper over. It is the only unread indicator there is — a settings page without it is a page where an arriving channel_needs_reauth alert is invisible until you leave, and Plan & billing and the Library are both surfaces people sit on. ONE row, never two: `notifications_row` IS the row out of `nav_groups`, and the two branches below are exclusive.
